This is FlagKan, running live.
It puts a red, yellow or green light on every brand-new Solana coin —
then grades its own call in public, six hours later.
This is the live board.
New coins land here about a minute after they launch.
No refresh button: the server pushes every new coin over a WebSocket, live.
Each row shows the light, the confidence, price, liquidity,
and how much of the coin the biggest wallet holds.
Filter by light, or search any name or mint.
Open any coin for the full verdict.
The bar shows the model's odds for red, yellow and green.
Under Why are the on-chain signals: whale share, the next ten holders, first-minute buys and coin age,
read through Solami RPC. Price and liquidity come from the market feed.
Check six is Jev, TypeSafe's AI judge, looking for wallets that move together.
These are reference levels. The light itself comes from a LightGBM model
trained on our own graded history, plus hard rules.

About six hours after first sight, we check the same market feed again.
A coin is dead if its price fell ninety percent,
liquidity dropped under a thousand dollars, or nobody traded in the last hour.
This is where we stay honest.
About 97.4 percent of new coins are dead within six hours.
So calling everything red is already 97.4 percent right.
Accuracy isn't our skill — and we show that baseline next to every number.
Our skill is finding the few survivors.
